# Skye Lickteig
## Texts and Technology, PhD student - Anime and Cultural Understanding

### About me

Greetings and salutations, my name is Skye Lickteig. I am from Farmersburg, Indiana. My father never graduated from high school; my mother got her BA while I was in high school. I have three children, a fifteen-year-old who was born on May the 4th because Star Wars is amazing, her name is Raven. I have a six-year-old who will be seven on January 4th, her name is Leia. I have a five-year-old who will be six on September 4th, just kidding, he broke my streak, his birthday is on the fifteenth and his name is Ray. Leia and Ray were both born in Japan during the 7 years that I lived there. The younger two have only gone to school here in the states but Raven was able to experience all six years of elementary school in Japan. She is fluent reading, writing, and speaking, elementary level Japanese. I encourage her daily to continue her studies to avoid losing her Japanese language skills.

Culture is my passion, I love to learn about culture, share culture, and teach about culture. I taught English for five years in Japan after I completed my degree at Kyushu University. During that time, I realized how important it is to have open minded people that are willing to question and be questioned. I also learned that there is a troubling amount of incorrect information in the world on culture from "foreign" places. 

I am grateful that although I hated primary education with a passion, I chose to try my luck at Valencia college in my twenties. I swore I'd never go to college, and now here I am working on a PhD! I have a BA in Classical Humanities and a MA in Japanese Humanities with a focus on text to technology adaptations via historical representation contradicted with modern film. My love for culture and my innate curiosity and desire to share have spurred my desire to keep pushing forward. I want to learn more about adaptations, cultural representations, and pedagogic applications for passing my knowledge on to others. 

My fervent hope is that my research will continue where it has left off. During my BA, my final project was a lower-level thesis which I wrote on the adaptation of Disney Princesses into Heroines, primarily beginning after the introduction of Ghibli films in Disney animations. I had no idea this would lead to a continuation of my interest in Ghibli films, but this time it was adaptations within a Ghibli film rather than caused by a Ghibli film. I would like to take another step and use Ghibli films to attempt to show the cultural norms that are in so many Japanese animations. 

While in Japan, I had many individuals mention to me that they visited the USA during a school exchange program but schools here were nothing like they are in American television shows. It was not until I had spent a few years in Japan and began teaching in actual elementary schools that I began to understand. While the plotlines are fantastical and there are no actual "magic girls" in Japanese schools, the everyday that is in the background of Japanese animation is a very realistic representation of living Japanese culture. 

My research will be geared towards finding or creating a pedagogic means for using that realistic cultural representation to share Japanese culture with anyone who is interested in learning about it, and hopefully, that will be foundational in a ripple effect of teaching culture via modern film technologies.
